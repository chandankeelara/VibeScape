      S1 · Calm — design notes

      1. Art is the loudest thing. A big rounded cover on the left, info on the right, a lot of space between. The background is one static, low-alpha gradient taken from the cover's colours. Two layers crossfade on track change, with no blur and no animation.
      2. Controls weigh almost nothing. Play, previous and next are bare glyphs that get a soft disc on hover, like Apple Music. When you pause, the cover eases back to 90% scale instead of the UI snapping, so you can see the state from across the room.
      3. The accent belongs to the vibe and nothing else. It's interpolated from the five band colours (cool slate at sleep to ember at beast) and only appears on the slider fill, the band word, the vibe badge, the equalizer, the DJ switch and focus rings. Text in the accent is automatically mixed toward white or black until it passes 4.5:1, and fills until they pass 3:1, in both themes.
      4. The vibe slider is the one signature element. It's a slim native range input (so keyboard and screen readers work) over a hand-drawn rail with detents at each band boundary. The five band names sit under their bands and are buttons that jump to the band's centre. PgUp/PgDn jumps a band.
      5. The DJ explains itself in one line, and Bit is that line's face. Bit hops when a track finishes and slumps after a skip, then goes back to neutral. It can be switched off in the account menu, and the line stays.
      6. Video is one surface whose layout only changes through CSS. Normal video widens the left column. Theater makes the video full width and moves meta, transport and vibe into one row underneath. The element is never moved or re-mounted.
      7. Phone: art, meta, transport and vibe all fit in one screen. Queue, recommendations and recent plays live in a bottom sheet you open from an "Up next" bar, the way every music app does it.
