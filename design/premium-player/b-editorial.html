Editorial Hardware

A precision instrument, printed like a magazine.

  1. The knob is the product. A 270° rotary with 51 printed ticks, a lit arc, and magnetic detents at every band boundary — 20/40/60/80 — that click the cap as you cross them. Drag it, scroll it, click the scale, use the band keys or the arrow keys.
  2. One accent, owned by the vibe. It travels blue → cyan → chartreuse → amber → red in OKLCH, so lightness stays even while hue does the talking. Nothing else on the page may have a colour.
  3. Numerals are graphics. Doto, a dot-matrix face, is reserved for live readouts only: the vibe LCD over its unlit 888 ghost, the clock, the counts. If it's in dots, it's moving.
  4. Depth by rim light, not shadow. On near-black a shadow is invisible, so keys are lifted by a lit top edge and pressed by losing it. The sleeve lights the table under it in the vibe's colour.
  5. Swiss grid, printed. Twelve faint column guides, crop marks on the sleeve, a folio line, numbered sections. Alignment is the ornament.
  6. Instruments tell the truth about energy. A ballistic VU needle and a dot-matrix spectrum swing harder and faster as the vibe rises; tempo is derived from the vibe, so the whole room breathes in time.
  7. Bit lives on the display. A 11×10 dot robot: bounces when a song finishes, sulks on an early skip, scans while the DJ is choosing, sleeps on pause.
  8. Video never moves. Sleeve and video share one hero node; theater only rewrites grid areas on the deck. In React that keeps the YouTube iframe mounted for life.
