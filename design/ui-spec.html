VibeScape — interface specification · rev 02 · 2026-10-02

The record supplies the colour. The chrome stays out of the way.

VibeScape should feel like a well-made piece of listening equipment: a dark instrument face, legible at a glance, where the only thing that changes colour is the music currently playing. Everything here follows from three positions — and from one physical model, a single light, set out in section 01 and obeyed everywhere.

  1. i — monochrome chrome, lit

    Every surface, rule, label and control is a grey — but a grey under a light, with a lit top edge and a dark bottom one. The mood accent gets exactly one appearance per region. A second accent in the same region is a bug, not emphasis.

  2. ii — type does the structuring

    Hierarchy comes from size, weight, case and a 1px rule — not from cards, not from shadows, not from a border radius. Three typefaces with three jobs: grotesk for interface, mono for data, serif once per screen.

  3. iii — every mark carries information

    A pill is a count or an action. The stack is the queue. The ring is the playhead. The breath is the tempo. Delight is allowed, but never on its own: if a mark has no referent it is removed and its space is kept.

Scope of rev 02 The player, in three directions, plus the material and motion model they share. The queue, sync and landing mocks are carried over from rev 01 unchanged and will be revisited.

01

The room

Material, light and motion. One model, stated once, obeyed everywhere.

Restraint on its own reads as austere. What separates expensive dark interfaces from cheap ones is not fewer marks — it is that the marks behave like material under a light. So the product gets a physical model, and everything obeys it.

01.1   the light

One source, above and slightly in front, at 118°. Top and left edges catch it; bottom and right edges fall away. A recess inverts: dark at the top lip, lit at the bottom. The critical consequence on a #08080c page is that a shadow cannot lift anything — it is invisible against near-black. Depth is carried by the edge; the shadow’s only remaining job is to kill the surface directly underneath an object.

There is exactly one violation, and it is the design position made physical: the record is the other light in the room. It lights itself from below, into a pool. Nothing else in the product is ever lit from beneath.

01.3   motion has mass
rule
1Input is acknowledged immediately; the consequence resolves later. A press responds within --dur-press (90ms) with a 1px depression and a tone change. What it causes takes --dur or longer. Reversing this is what makes an interface feel laggy even when it is fast.
2Things settle, they do not stop. Entrances use --ease-out, whose long tail is the settle. No bounce and no overshoot — with one exception, below.

01.4   the breath

The app knows the tempo and the energy of every track and currently acknowledges neither. The restrained version of that is not a visualiser. Exactly two elements move with the music, and they move on the metadata, not on the AnalyserNode: the light pool under the sleeve, and the 9px square in the wordmark — a tally light.

tokenvaluenote
--beatcalc(60s / bpm)Already written for the Bit rig. Unknown BPM means the breath is off — never guessed at 120.
--energy0 … 1From the same analysis row as the vibe.
--breathcalc(0.04 + 0.16 × var(--energy))Depth of the pulse: ±4% for a ballad, ±18% for a beast track. Tempo sets the speed, energy sets the size — the same split the mascot’s groove bands use.
envelope0% peak → 55% floor → rest, --ease-outPercussive: instant attack on the beat, decay across the bar. A sine wave reads as a lava lamp.

Why metadata beats the analyser: it is phase-locked rather than jittery, correct through an ambient intro, costs zero per-frame JavaScript, and runs on the compositor. A visualiser reacts; this one knows. Hard rule: only ever two. Adding a third means removing one. The breath pauses during a track change and restarts in phase with the new track at t = 640ms, or the old tempo bleeds into the new record.

01.5   three signature moments

Nobody loves a system. They love three or four specific moments. Each of these also tells the user something true — that is the test that keeps them out of the decoration bin.

A — dragging the dial drags the light

applyAccent() fires on input, not on change. The entire room — pool, play ring, slider fill, every accent in the interface — follows the thumb in real time. Dragging the control is dragging a light across the product. Three layers make it feel mechanical rather than reactive:

layertimingbehaviour
live re-light0msAccent tracks the pointer with no transition. A transition here would feel like drag on the control itself.
labels lead--dur-fastA band name lifts from --text-mute to --text-3 about two units before the thumb arrives. Anticipation is what makes a control feel like it has a mind.
thumb settles260msOn release the rendered thumb overshoots by 1.5% of travel and decays back. The committed value never overshoots — only the paint has inertia. Implement it on the thumb’s transform, never on the value.
the road aheadstaticThe unfilled groove holds the full spectrum at 18%, not grey. You can always see where you are going.
B — the stack advances

The track change, 640 ms total, overlapping. The order is the whole trick: the image changes, then the room catches up. Reversed, it looks like a bug.

twhatwhy
0Outgoing sleeve drops 10px and fades out, 320ms --ease-in.It falls out of its own pool, which is below it. That is what reads as “finished” instead of “slid away”.
0Pool dims to 30% and recovers by 640ms.The room takes one breath. It is also the only moment the pool is allowed to stop breathing.
120Stack advances: both sheets rise 6px; the new hero rises from +6px and fades in, 420ms --ease-out.Overlap at 40% per rule 3. The sheets moving is what makes the queue feel physical.
120Title and liner notes crossfade, --dur, with a 1px baseline rise.Type never travels (rule 6). 1px is the whole allowance.
120–640Accent crossfades across all four tokens, --dur-art.The slowest element, so the room finishes re-lighting after the art has settled.
640Breath restarts in phase at the new --beat.—
reducedNo transforms at all: 200ms opacity crossfade on art and title, instant accent swap, pool stays at full.The resting state is readable at every point, which dropping the animation alone would not give.

Custom properties only animate if registered. The four accent tokens need @property with syntax: ‘<color>’, or this is a hard cut whatever duration is set.

C — the ring is the playhead

A 2px ring, 7px off the play disc, filled clockwise from the top by a conic-gradient in --vibe-accent-text. The most looked-at and most pressed object in the app also tells you where you are in the track — a watch-face move, and the reason the numeric remaining-time can go.

The redundancy is resolved deliberately: the ring is for knowing, the linear bar is for seeking — you cannot scrub a 54px ring accurately. So the bar sits at 40% opacity at rest and comes to full on hover or focus of the transport region. Less chrome while you listen, the full control the moment you reach for it. The disc itself is machined: lit top inset, dark bottom inset, --cast-3, and on press it travels 1px down while the cast tightens to --contact over 90ms. It goes into the page.

would Braun have done this The test every addition had to pass. It is what killed a gloss highlight on the sleeve, a bounce on the slider, a glow on hover, and an amplitude-reactive pool. It is what kept four edge lines, one wash, and a tally light.
premium is not more effects Nothing here adds a layer that does not also carry information. The stack is the queue. The ring is the playhead. The breath is the tempo. The pool is the artwork. Remove the information and the mark goes with it.
the grain is load-bearing 2.2% dither is not styling. The pools are low-alpha radial gradients and they band into visible rings on 8-bit panels; this removes the banding. If the pools ever go, so does the grain.
what still never moves The top bar, the search field, the grid, the queue rail, and all type. The entire motion budget of the player is: the breath, the track change, the dial, and press feedback.
02

Player — three directions

Same shell, same tokens, same queue budget. Three different answers to where the weight sits.

direction the idea weight sits the sleeve the dial gives up
A — The Press The record is the object; the interface is its label. Left, on the art. 420px, stacked, pooled. Dominant. A quiet rule at the foot of the meta column. Dial discoverability; the vertical recent trail; meta column width.
B — The Console The dial is the instrument; everything else reports to it. Far left, on the fader. 300px. A readout, not the hero. A full-height channel-strip fader with a printed scale. Artwork scale; it is the busiest of the three; the trail shrinks to 24px.
C — The Session The screen is a timeline; the track is where you are in it. Low and wide, on the ground line. 310px, standing on the line, flanked by past and future. A full-width ruler across the top — the weather for the session. Vertical balance; liner density (6 facts → 3); it makes the queue rail a list rather than a preview.
held constant Accent contract, type scale, spacing grid, pill geometry, the 320px rail and its 5‑row / 3‑row budget, and every prohibition. Only composition varies.
each gives something up A direction that costs nothing has not chosen anything. The trade-offs above are the actual decision.

07

Reference

The system itself. Everything above is assembled only from what follows.

07.1   type scale — seven steps
--fs-micro  10pxup next   dj picks   104 bpmmono labels, counts, timecodes, table heads. Always uppercase, always 0.14em.
--fs-meta  11.5px212 tracks · Chandan Keelararow metadata, pill detail lines, margin notes. Mono or sans.
--fs-sm  13pxTime Moves Slow — BADBADNOTGOODlist row titles, secondary body, buttons, hints.
--fs-body  15pxKhruangbin / Mordechaithe default. Artist line, paragraphs, anything read in sentences.
--fs-title  19pxAdd musicmodal and panel titles, the wordmark.
--fs-display  28pxSo We Won’t Forgetthe track title, and the two stat numerals in sync.
--fs-hero  44–64pxOne diallanding headline only. clamp(44px, 5vw, 64px).
what was wrong 229 hardcoded font-size declarations across the next app, 25 distinct values, 16 of them inside the 9–17px band. Four sizes between 9.5 and 11.5px cannot be told apart on screen but can all be got wrong.
why it accelerates 10 → 11.5 → 13 → 15 is roughly ×1.15; 15 → 19 → 28 → 44 is ×1.27 to ×1.57. Tight at the bottom where the job is legibility, open at the top where the job is expression. A single uniform ratio gives either unreadable captions or a timid headline.
line height 1.3 for the two display steps, 1.35 for row titles, 1.6–1.7 for anything at --fs-sm and below. Set with the step; never overridden per component.
migration 9/9.5/10/10.5 → --fs-micro. 11/11.5/12/12.5 → --fs-meta. 13/13.5/14 → --fs-sm. 14.5/15/16/17 → --fs-body. 18/20/22 → --fs-title. 24/26/28/32 → --fs-display. 38/44 → --fs-hero.
07.2   the accent contract

--vibe-accent is written by applyAccent() on every track change and is unknowable at authoring time. Measured across the ten band endpoints in lib/vibe.js, its contrast on --bg swings from 3.02:1 (sleep, low end) to 9.34:1 (chill, high end). No fixed foreground is safe against that, and switching black/white ink per track still bottoms out at 4.32:1 — below AA. So the raw accent is not permitted to carry or back text at all. Two derived tones are, and both are computed once per track change.

tokenmay be used formust neverworst / best
--vibe-accent Non-text marks only: the 2px transport fill, the slider fill, the sync-pill edge bar, the art light pool, the queue-row hover inset rule, the wordmark square, color-mix washes at ≤14%. // lib/vibe.js — the contract, computed once per track change.
// The artwork picks the hue. It does not get to pick legibility.
const OKL_TEXT = 0.74;  // measured: >= 6.93:1 on --bg across all 10 band endpoints
const OKL_FILL = 0.82;  // measured: >= 8.23:1 for --bg ink sitting on the fill

export function applyAccent(vibe) {
  const { a, b } = accentFor(vibe);
  const root = document.documentElement;
  root.style.setProperty('--vibe-accent',   a);
  root.style.setProperty('--vibe-accent-2', b);
  // Derived, not decorative. Every accent that touches text uses one of these.
  root.style.setProperty('--vibe-accent-text', liftL(a, OKL_TEXT));
  root.style.setProperty('--vibe-accent-fill', liftL(a, OKL_FILL));
}

why not switch the ink Picking black or white per track by luminance was the obvious fix and it fails: beast’s high endpoint rgb(220,38,38) gives 4.14:1 against --bg and 4.32:1 against --text. Its best possible ink still misses AA. Lifting the fill is the only move that always works.
focus rings outline: 2px solid var(--vibe-accent-text) with outline-offset: 2px. Never the raw accent — at 3.02:1 a focus ring on --bg would be invisible on the sleep band.
washes color-mix(in oklab, var(--vibe-accent) 12%, transparent) over --surface moves the bed by less than 0.3 of a contrast point, so text on it keeps its ramp rating. 14% is the ceiling; past that the bed must be re-measured.
reduced motion The re-light between tracks is a --dur-art transition on the four custom properties. Under prefers-reduced-motion it becomes an instant swap — the resting state is the new accent, fully applied, never a half-faded one.

the --text-4 problem At 30% it measured 2.42:1 — a third of the way to AA — and it is load-bearing: 57 references across 15 stylesheets, carrying the sync-pill detail line, QueueRow .meta, .emptyHint, SyncModal .rowMeta / .footerMeta / .urlHint, every mono label and every placeholder.
why the whole ramp moved Lifting only --text-4 to .50 would have placed it within 0.3 of a contrast point of --text-3 at .48 — two tokens no one could tell apart. The ramp is re-spaced to .78 / .62 / .50 so the four steps stay visibly distinct while all four pass.
migration About 22 of the 57 references are SVG stroke and fill values in bit/animations.module.css, which were chosen for 30% and documented as such. Those move to --text-mute unchanged. The remainder keep --text-4 and simply get brighter.
one new rule --text-mute may not appear in a color declaration. It exists so a non-text mark still has a legitimate home at 30%, and so a lint can reject the one use that matters.

seven geometries, one shape Measured in the running stylesheets: heights of 20, 22, 28 and “whatever padding makes”, paddings of 7, 8, 9 and 10, and two different font sizes. They all look like the same object and none of them line up on a shared baseline.

These are the values. The behaviour — what accelerates, what settles, the per-component state table and the three signature choreographies — is in 01.3 and 01.5. The material tokens (--edge-lit, --edge-dark, --contact, --cast-3/4, --sheen, --vignette, --grain-alpha) and the breath (--beat, --energy, --breath) are in 01.1 and 01.4.

Nothing in this specification moves on load. There is no stagger, no reveal and no scroll-triggered entrance; the only motion in the product is a response to an action, plus the per-track re-light. Every animation carries a prefers-reduced-motion branch, and that branch must leave a readable resting state — for the re-light, the new accent fully applied; for the sync pill, present and still.

why radii came down 10 / 14 / 20 on a product whose largest radius sits on a 560px card is a soft, consumer-app read. 6 / 10 / 16 keeps the softness where the hand is — small controls — and lets the big surfaces look machined.
the duplicate --radius-card and --radius-lg are both 20px today and mean the same thing. Two names for one value is how a scale starts drifting.
the accent transition Custom properties are only animatable if registered. Register the four accent tokens with @property and syntax: ‘<color>’, or the re-light is a hard cut whatever duration is set.

Section head Mono title + optional tag + optional action pill, 22px min-height, sits directly above its list with --sp-2.

Progress Two representations, one value: the ring around the play disc is for knowing (always on), the 2px linear bar is for seeking (40% at rest, full on hover or focus of the transport region). See 01.5-C.

Mood dial Two forms, one behaviour (01.5-A): a 4px horizontal groove (A, C) or a 408px vertical fader with a printed scale and a machined cap (B). Both are recessed (elev-1), both hold the full spectrum at 18% ahead of the value, both re-light the room live on input.

Segmented control 1px bordered, --radius-sm, active segment on --surface-2 with --text. Audio/video only.

Checkbox 15px, 3px radius, --border-strong. Checked: --vibe-accent-fill with a 2px --vibe-accent-ink tick.

Modal 560px, --radius-lg, --surface, rim light on the top border, one deep shadow, footer on --bg-2. Tabs are a 1px underline.

Stat --fs-display mono numeral over a .mlabel, separated by a hairline above. Used in both sync phases.

Empty state 1px dashed block, title at --fs-sm, one hint line at --fs-meta. No illustration, no call to action.

Sync pill 364px, --radius, rim light, one shadow, progress as a 2px bottom edge. The only floating component.

Focus ring 2px solid var(--vibe-accent-text), offset 2px, on every interactive element. Never removed, never the raw accent.

deliberately left alone The Bit mascot rig and its dock, the media layer and its imperative singletons, the queue row-budget arithmetic, the two-tab sync IA, useAutoMinimise, the mobile queue sheet, and the admin and metrics panels. None of those are what makes the product read as generic.
order of work Tokens first — the text ramp, the accent derivations, the type scale — because they change every screen at once and are testable on their own. Then the pill and the list row, which together account for most of the visual noise. Screens last.
